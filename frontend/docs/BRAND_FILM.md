# The search, reconsidered

The homepage opens with a 24-second, silent motion-graphics film. The story moves from repeated forms to a CV, matched jobs and applications at the user's chosen pace. Scrolling seeks the actual MP4 in either direction; translated HTML carries the narrative so it stays readable and accessible in English and Arabic. The imagery uses existing AutoLinky app screenshots, labeled as sample data.

`src/ScrollFilm.jsx` owns the timeline, playback, chapter controls, keyboard range and transcript. `src/brandFilm.js` owns the bilingual story. The section uses ordinary document scrolling and a sticky stage; it does not capture wheel/touch input. Skip film moves focus to the next section. Reduced motion and short landscape viewports remove the long pinned section and expose the transcript. Missing or unsupported media retains the poster, signup action and readable story, including errors that happen before hydration. Without JavaScript, the poster and transcript remain available without a long pinned section.

The movie is original programmatic motion graphics, rendered from `scripts/render-brand-film.mjs`; it is not AI-generated live-action footage. Runway was discovered but its account is not connected, so no Runway video was generated. No external footage, fabricated product screens or new icon pack is used. A sourced photorealistic iPhone hardware frame replaces the earlier drawn outline; actual safe-area app renders sit behind it. [Frame provenance, shared geometry and screen reproduction](DEVICE_MOCKUP.md). Film labels and app imagery are in English; the accompanying narrative and controls are localized.

The opening paperwork uses complete, typeset sample documents instead of schematic cards and placeholder bars: a CV, a cover letter and three different application forms repeat the same candidate's details. A generated blank paper material supplies subtle fibers and lighting; the documents are neither photographs nor scans of a real person's paperwork. `scripts/film-document-content.json` keeps their text editable, while `scripts/film-documents.mjs` renders five A4 WebP assets at 2000 × 2828 from a 1000 × 1414 logical layout. The source material lives at `scripts/assets/paper-sheet.png`; the renderer crops its flat interior to exclude the generated cutout fringe. All candidate details, employers and education are fictional, use a reserved example.com contact address and carry a small sample-document footer. [Document provenance, layout and reproduction](FILM_DOCUMENTS.md).

The shipped media is `public/assets/film/autolinky-story.mp4` (1080 × 1080, 60 fps, H.264, no audio) and its WebP poster. Fast-start metadata and a keyframe every 100 milliseconds keep seeking practical. The player preloads on normal connections and loads metadata only for slow connections, Save-Data or reduced motion; there is no autoplay. The player URL includes the `continuous-story-1` media revision to invalidate earlier cached footage. `server.js` serves MP4/WebM MIME types, byte ranges, suffix/open ranges, HEAD and unsatisfiable-range responses so browser seeking also works on the Node production server.

To reproduce, run from `frontend/` with Node, an optional installation of `@napi-rs/canvas` (validated with 0.1.100) and FFmpeg (validated with 7.1):

```text
node scripts/render-brand-film.mjs --modules <absolute-node_modules-directory> --ffmpeg <absolute-ffmpeg-executable>
```

The renderer uses the bundled Manrope font, prepared safe-area images in `public/assets/app/iphone/`, and the raster hardware/aperture from `src/iphoneMockup.json`. Its document helper combines the editable content and blank material into `public/assets/film/documents/*.webp` before rendering the film. The detail callout uses the refreshed unframed matches image. Its source rectangle is (48, 686, 690, 556), covering the complete first job card, including missing skills, both actions and the bottom border. Its 386-pixel display width determines the 311.04-pixel height from that crop aspect ratio, so it is not stretched or clipped. The card lifts from and returns to (72, 1206, 1035, 834) in the safe-area phone image; that image has a different vertical inset from the unframed source. Optional render tools stay outside production dependencies. It writes the movie/poster to public assets and inspection stills into ignored `output/playwright/film-stills/`. Editing the translated HTML narrative does not require rerendering the video. Editing document text, material or layout requires rebuilding its images and the film. The 24-second duration and 60 fps timeline are preserved.

`scripts/film-motion.mjs` defines one absolute-time choreography. At 4 seconds the CV pulls back while the application arrives; at 8 the form leaves and the phone arrives beside the retained CV. At 12 the same handset slides from profile to matches inside its aperture, then lifts its first complete card. At 16 that card has returned before preferences slide in. At 20 the settings handset fans right, revealing matches in the middle and profile on the left. These are object handoffs rather than whole-scene dissolves. Seeking backward retraces the same geometry. The renderer checks pixel-identical return visits at all five boundaries. `--preview-only` includes handoff samples as well as the paper close-ups. The HTML story uses the same progress value for a 0.7-second fade-through and short vertical travel, with one stationary story for reduced motion or media failure. Story blocks keep a common footprint so the controls and CTA do not jump.

The branding update refreshes the four product images from actual Flutter widget renders with deterministic sample data: `06_matches_en`, `07_profile_en`, `43_auto_apply_preferences_en` and `24_explore_en` in the shared app's screenshot suite. They are converted to WebP, and the film is rerendered with the current mobile palette. These fixtures do not represent live accounts, jobs or outcomes.

Validation and release state are recorded in `WORKSPACE_DESIGN.md` and the repository `PLAN.md`. Local verification does not mean the website has been deployed.
